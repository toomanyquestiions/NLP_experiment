Water on Earth is constantly moving between the oceans, the air and the land. The sun heats the surface of seas and lakes, and some of the water turns into vapor. Rising vapor cools and forms clouds made of tiny droplets. When the droplets grow heavy, they fall as rain or snow. Part of this water flows into rivers, and part soaks into the ground. This endless circulation is called the water cycle.

Honeybees live in large family groups called colonies. Worker bees fly from flower to flower to collect nectar and pollen. While moving between plants, they carry pollen on their bodies and help flowers produce seeds. Many fruits and vegetables depend on this pollination. In the hive, nectar is turned into honey and stored in wax cells. A single colony can contain tens of thousands of bees.

Plants make their own food through a process called photosynthesis. Their green leaves contain chlorophyll, which captures energy from sunlight. Using this energy, the plant turns water and carbon dioxide into sugar. Oxygen is released into the air as a by-product. Animals and people breathe this oxygen. For this reason, forests are often called the lungs of the planet.

The Moon circles the Earth about once every month. Its gravity pulls on the oceans and creates tides. Most coasts have two high tides and two low tides each day. The Sun also affects tides, but its pull is weaker than that of the Moon. When the Sun, Earth and Moon line up, tides become especially strong. Sailors have used tide tables for centuries to plan their voyages.

Around 1450, Johannes Gutenberg built a printing press with movable metal type in Europe. Before that, books were copied by hand, which took months and made them very expensive. The new machine allowed many identical pages to be printed quickly. Books became cheaper, and more people learned to read. Ideas and discoveries began to spread across countries much faster. Many historians regard this invention as a turning point in human history.

A volcano is an opening in the Earth's crust through which hot rock, ash and gases escape. Under the surface, molten rock called magma collects in large chambers. When pressure grows too high, it can push upward and cause an eruption. After it reaches the surface, magma is called lava. Eruptions can be dangerous, but volcanic ash also makes soil very fertile. Scientists monitor volcanoes with instruments to warn people in time.

Sleep is essential for the health of the body and the brain. During the night, a person goes through several cycles of light and deep sleep. In deep sleep the body repairs tissues and strengthens the immune system. During other phases, the brain organizes memories from the day. Most adults need between seven and nine hours of sleep. Regular lack of sleep can reduce attention and worsen mood.

Paper is made from wood fibers, so producing it requires trees and a lot of water. Recycling used paper reduces the need to cut down forests. At a recycling plant, old paper is mixed with water and turned into pulp. The pulp is cleaned, pressed and dried into new sheets. Paper fibers can be reused several times before they become too short. Sorting waste paper at home makes this process easier.

The Silk Road was a network of trade routes connecting East Asia with the Mediterranean. Caravans carried silk, spices, paper and many other goods across deserts and mountains. Cities along the route, such as Samarkand and Turkistan, grew rich from trade. Merchants also exchanged ideas, religions and technologies. Travel was slow, and few traders covered the whole route themselves. The routes gradually lost importance after sea transport became cheaper.

A library is a place where books and other materials are collected and shared. Readers can borrow items for a limited time, usually free of charge. Today, many libraries also offer computers, quiet study rooms and lectures. Some of them keep rare manuscripts that are hundreds of years old. Librarians help visitors find reliable information. Digital catalogs make it possible to search a collection from home.

The bicycle is one of the most efficient ways for a person to travel. A cyclist uses much less energy per kilometer than a walker. Bicycles produce no exhaust, so they do not pollute the air. In many cities, special lanes make cycling safer. Regular riding strengthens the heart and the legs. Wearing a helmet greatly reduces the risk of head injury.

The outer layer of the Earth is broken into large pieces called tectonic plates. These plates move very slowly, only a few centimeters per year. Sometimes they get stuck, and stress builds up along their edges. When the rock suddenly slips, the ground shakes in an earthquake. Strong earthquakes are measured with seismographs. Buildings designed to resist shaking are far safer in earthquake zones.

Solar panels turn sunlight directly into electricity. They are made of cells that release electric current when light hits them. Panels can be placed on roofs or in large fields called solar farms. They work best in sunny regions, but also produce some power under clouds. Solar energy does not release carbon dioxide while operating. Batteries are often added to store electricity for the evening.

The Aral Sea lies between Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan. It was once one of the largest lakes in the world. Beginning in the 1960s, much of the water of the rivers feeding it was diverted for irrigation. As a result, the sea shrank dramatically and its water became much saltier. Fishing towns on the old shore lost their main source of income. In recent years, a dam in the northern part has helped restore part of the Small Aral Sea.

The number zero was developed over many centuries. Ancient Indian mathematicians treated zero as a number with its own rules. Later, scholars in the Arab world helped spread it to Europe. Zero made it possible to write large numbers using only ten digits. Without it, modern arithmetic and computers would be very hard to imagine. Today the idea of nothing is one of the foundations of mathematics.

A glacier is a large mass of ice that moves slowly under its own weight. It forms where more snow falls in winter than melts in summer. Over many years, the snow is pressed into dense ice. Glaciers store a large share of the planet's fresh water. In mountain regions they feed rivers during the warm season. Scientists measure their size to track changes in climate.

Railways allow heavy loads to be moved over long distances using relatively little energy. Steel wheels roll on steel rails with very little friction. The first steam locomotives appeared in the early nineteenth century. Today many lines use electric trains, which are quieter and cleaner. High-speed trains can travel at more than 250 kilometers per hour. Kazakhstan has a long railway network that links its distant regions.

A vaccine teaches the body to recognize a disease without causing the illness itself. It contains a weakened or inactive part of a germ, or instructions for making a harmless piece of it. The immune system learns to respond and remembers the germ. If the person later meets the real germ, the body can react quickly. Vaccination has helped almost eliminate several serious diseases. Doctors recommend following the official vaccination schedule.

The dombra is a traditional two-stringed musical instrument of the Kazakh people. It has a long neck and a pear-shaped wooden body. The player plucks or strums the strings with the right hand. Many folk songs and instrumental pieces called kuis are performed on it. Kuis often tell stories about nature, history or the feelings of their composers. Children in many Kazakh schools learn to play the dombra.

Nauryz is a spring holiday celebrated in Kazakhstan around the spring equinox, on March 21 to 23. It marks the beginning of a new year in the traditional calendar of many peoples of Central Asia. Families clean their houses and visit relatives and neighbors. A special dish called nauryz kozhe is prepared from seven ingredients. Concerts, folk games and fairs are held in squares and parks. The holiday symbolizes renewal, kindness and friendship.
